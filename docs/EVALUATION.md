# Evaluation

Generated by `backend/scripts/evaluate.py` on the CC0 fixtures in `samples/` (`fixtures.py`), whose ground truth is exact. The scored path is the production pipeline: HTDemucs, mixture-silence gate, source discovery, policy engine, note refinement and cross-source attribution. **Raw** is the engine output; **refined** is what the app shows. The fixtures are synthetic: they check behaviour (absence, quiet and repeated notes, silence, reverb, chunk boundaries) and do not predict accuracy on commercial recordings.

## BabySlakh benchmark (realistic multi-instrument songs)

BabySlakh (Manilow et al., WASPAA 2019; CC BY 4.0) is 20 songs rendered from MIDI with professional
sample libraries, with every instrument's audio and MIDI. Each song is scored on a 30 s window
starting at 60 s. Reproduce with:

```bash
python scripts/evaluate_slakh.py --root <BabySlakh>/babyslakh_16k --engines policy transkun yourmt3-mix muscriptor     --oracle-engines policy transkun --json results.json      # about 3.5 h on a 16-thread CPU
python scripts/summarize_slakh.py results.json                # the tables below
python scripts/rescore_slakh.py --root <BabySlakh>/babyslakh_16k results.json   # hybrid policies
```

Three corrections were needed before the numbers meant anything, and each was measured:

* **Bass references are an octave too high.** In 18 of 20 songs the bass sample library sounds 12
  semitones below its MIDI (pYIN: written median 52-55, sounding 40-43). Transcribers report sounding
  pitch, which is also what a General MIDI bass needs, so bass references are moved to sounding pitch
  (YourMT3+ on a true bass stem: F1 0.00 against the written MIDI, 0.99 after the shift). Other stems
  are not shifted: in polyphony the pYIN estimate is unreliable, and transcribers already match them.
* **Families come from the GM program, not Slakh's class label.** "Strings (continued)" also holds
  synth strings, Choir Aahs and orchestra hits (programs 50-55).
* **The audio is 16 kHz.** Band-limiting the demo the same way drops our hi-hat F1 from 1.00 to 0.57
  (kick and snare unchanged), so the hi-hat number below understates full-band performance.

MuScriptor was attempted on every song and reported its gated-weights error; it has no numbers here.

BabySlakh tracks scored: 20 (30 s window from 60 s, 16 kHz source audio).

### Choosing an engine per track by render fidelity: rejected

Render fidelity needs no ground truth, so it was tested as a way to pick the better engine per source
(`scripts/select_by_render.py`): render both engines' MIDI, keep the one whose render matches the
source's own audio better (chroma + onset correlation), then score the choice against the true part.

| Per-source choice (53 sources, 20 songs) | Median F1 vs the true part | Mean F1 |
|---|---|---|
| Always Basic Pitch | 0.306 | 0.351 |
| Always YourMT3+ (mix, attributed) | 0.463 | 0.435 |
| Selected by render fidelity | 0.351 | 0.372 |
| Best possible choice (uses ground truth) | 0.478 | 0.465 |

It picked the more accurate engine for only 25 of 53 sources, mostly preferring Basic Pitch, which
transcribes everything audible in a separated source, bleed included, and therefore re-renders closer
to it. Render fidelity describes how well a MIDI file reproduces the separated stem; it is **not** a
measure of transcription accuracy, and the app does not use it to choose engines.

### What the numbers say

* **Separation is the bottleneck in dense mixes.** Transcribers do far better on the true stems
  (Basic Pitch piano 0.75, Transkun piano 0.97) than on separated sources (0.34 and 0.43). Drums and
  bass separate well (SI-SDR 10.8 and 5.9 dB); `htdemucs_6s` guitar and piano do not (-3.8 and -4.2 dB).
* **Mix-level transcription beats per-stem transcription** here: YourMT3+ on the whole mix with notes
  assigned to sources scored higher on every song (20 of 20); onset+offset F1 rose from 0.15 to 0.78.
* **Basic Pitch fails on sustained, soft-attack parts** even on true stems: strings 0.07, synth pads
  and leads 0.00, brass 0.02. Those parts need the multi-instrument models.
* **Velocity** from the stem's loudness correlates weakly with the true velocities (r 0.37-0.58).
  Transkun's own velocities do better on piano (r 0.65). A pitch-specific constant-Q estimate was tried
  and was worse (piano r 0.45 vs 0.64), so it was not adopted.
* **MIDI cannot carry the sound.** Rendering the *true* MIDI through the soundfont and comparing it with
  the true audio leaves an 8.4 dB spectral difference and 0.89 rhythm correlation. That is the floor for
  any Soundtrap instrument choice, before a single note is wrong.

## Design decisions the numbers drove

* **Split-sustain merge threshold.** Joining a note to the previous same-pitch note when the CQT energy at that pitch does not rise: a rise ratio of 1.2 merged 25 fragments on the demo with no recall loss; 2.0 merged 52 and dropped bass recall to 0.91 by joining real repeated eighth notes. 1.2 ships.
* **Cross-source attribution ratio.** Removing a note that another source has ≥ k× louder: k = 1.0 gave the best union F1 (0.82) but moved keys notes into the wrong source; k = 1.5 gave 0.765 with recall 1.0 and better per-source F1. 1.5 ships.
* **Harmonic-ghost filter: not shipped.** Suppressing notes 12/19/24/28 semitones above a louder simultaneous note gained at most +0.03 F1. Classifying the demo's false positives showed why: most were re-attacks inside held notes (57) and cross-source duplicates (35), not harmonics (2).
* **Bleed reattribution by envelope or chroma: not shipped.** In the drums+bass fixture the bass's upper harmonics leak into the residual (−19 dB; tagged brass/didgeridoo, so no fake brass track was created). Neither frame-energy correlation (−0.09 with the bass, 0.73 between unrelated stems elsewhere) nor chroma similarity (0.80 bass vs 0.84 drums) identified the owner, so that audio stays in the downloadable unattributed residual instead of being guessed into a track.

## Known gaps these fixtures show

* Refinement is not free on every part: demo bass recall goes 0.989 → 0.966 (2 of 88 notes) while precision rises 0.845 → 0.876, from the bass octave check and cross-source attribution together. The raw notes are kept per track, and octave moves are reversible in the editor.

* Onset+offset F1 is low wherever notes ring (keys with reverb: 0.07). Note ends follow the decay the model hears, not the key release; offsets are not corrected.
* Precision on the residual "lead" source stays low (≈ 0.3): it holds the lead plus keys bleed, and Basic Pitch transcribes both.
* Velocity is relative loudness from the stem's RMS. It preserves dynamics (loud minus quiet ≈ +28 on the keys fixture) but is not calibrated to the performer's velocity.
* All fixtures are synthetic. No licensed, annotated real-world multitrack set was available in this environment, so accuracy on commercial recordings is unmeasured here. The real-model checks that ran on actual model outputs are in `backend/tests/` (padded-demo regression, full pipeline).
